Gloss vs an AI assistant

An AI assistant can explain a word in context. It can also ask you to sign up, hand over an API key, wait for a network round trip, and read a paragraph in a chat window — every single time you meet a word you do not know.

Gloss An AI assistant
Answers in context Yes Yes — better, in fact
An account None One, and an API key
A network request per lookup None One, every time
Answers a word it has never seen Says so Usually
Works offline Yes No
What it costs you Nothing A subscription, and tokens
Time from selecting a word to reading it Instant Seconds, plus a copy and a paste

Where an AI assistant wins

On words no dictionary has — a coinage, a piece of jargon from your industry, a name you have not seen. It can often work it out from the sentence. Gloss says "not in the dictionary" and offers you a search, because it would rather be honest than clever.

Where Gloss wins

On the fourth word of a paragraph you are halfway through. An assistant wants a conversation; Gloss wants a panel. One of them is a thing you use twenty times a day while reading, and the other is a thing you use when you have already stopped reading.

They are not really rivals: Gloss for the words, an assistant for the sentences. But only one of them is instant, costs nothing to run, and works on a plane.
